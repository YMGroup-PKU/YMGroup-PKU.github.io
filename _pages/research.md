My group develops and applies high-fidelity simulation and modern machine learning to understand and predict complex turbulent flows. Our work spans the following directions.

High-fidelity turbulence simulation (DNS/LES)
======
We develop and apply high-fidelity direct numerical simulation (DNS) and large-eddy simulation (LES) for complex turbulent flows, leveraging GPU heterogeneous computing to extend the resolution, geometric complexity, and multiphysics that can be simulated faithfully.

Machine-learning and data-driven turbulence modeling
======
A central theme is data-driven and interpretable turbulence modeling. Using gene-expression programming, evolutionary neural networks, and modern deep-learning architectures, we build RANS and subgrid-scale closures with explicit, generalizable expressions, and pursue continual and mixture-of-experts modeling toward general frameworks.

Laminar–turbulent transition and wall turbulence
======
We study the mechanisms of laminar–turbulent transition and the structure of wall-bounded turbulence, including vortex-surface evolution, turbulent spots, and the construction of wall turbulence from hierarchically distributed hairpin vortices.

Turbomachinery and aero-engine internal flows
======
Combining high-fidelity simulation with model development, we investigate boundary-layer transition, separation, and loss generation in high- and low-pressure turbines and compressors relevant to aero-engine internal flows.

Interfacial instability, mixing, and frontier directions
======
We develop computable models for compressible interfacial-instability-induced turbulent mixing, and explore frontier directions such as quantum computing for fluid dynamics.
